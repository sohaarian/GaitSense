# Usability evaluation protocol: GaitRec Rehab Review prototype

**Purpose:** find out whether rehabilitation clinicians and nurse case managers can read the review page correctly, and whether they find it useful. This protocol tests understanding, not clinical effectiveness.

**Status:** designed, not yet run. Running it needs participants and, if results are to be published, review by an institutional ethics board.

## Participants

Five to eight people:

- at least two rehabilitation clinicians (physiotherapists or rehabilitation physicians);
- at least two nurse case managers who handle workers' compensation lower-limb injuries.

Five participants typically reveal most comprehension problems in a formative test. Participation is voluntary and unpaid or modestly compensated. No patient data beyond the de-identified GaitRec records are shown.

## Session (about 30 minutes, video call with screen share)

1. **Introduction (3 min).** Purpose, voluntary participation, no right or wrong answers, permission to take notes. No recording without explicit consent.
2. **Free exploration (4 min).** Think aloud while looking at the page.
3. **Comprehension tasks (15 min).** Use three pre-selected patients (one with no prompt, one with "persistent low loading", one with "loading above typical range"). For each:
    - What does the main chart show?
    - Has this patient's injured-leg loading changed since the first visit? How sure can you be?
    - What does the shaded band mean?
    - What would you need to check before acting on this page?
    - What does this page *not* tell you?
4. **Threshold task (3 min).** Switch between the corrected and original thresholds. Explain in your own words why the number of prompts changes.
5. **Questionnaire (5 min).** The 10-item System Usability Scale, plus three statements rated 1–5:
    - I understood what the review prompts mean.
    - I could tell the difference between a real change and ordinary variation.
    - This page could help me decide which cases to discuss with the treating clinician.

## What is measured

| Measure | Target that would justify further development |
|---|---|
| Correct answers on comprehension questions | At least 80% |
| Participants who read a prompt as "deterioration" or "not recovering" | None |
| Participants who state at least two things the page cannot tell them | All |
| System Usability Scale | Above 68, the commonly used average |

## Analysis

- Count errors and misreadings per question.
- Group comments into themes: clarity, trust, usefulness, risk of misuse.
- Every misreading that could lead to a wrong decision about a worker becomes a change to the page or a new entry in the hazard log.

## Risks and safeguards

- The page could be read as a judgement about a worker. The task "what does this page not tell you" tests this directly.
- Nurse case managers may face pressure to use any score in claim decisions. The intended-use statement is shown and discussed at the start, and the session records whether participants would use it that way.
